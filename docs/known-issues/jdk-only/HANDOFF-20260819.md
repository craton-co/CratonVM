## 1. Where the goal actually stands

The P0 table in [`jdk-only-runtime-services.md`](runtime-services-blocker-inventory.md)
is the completion condition. **Two of its rows are closed** (*Real boot-image
requirement*, `CLOSED(5)`; and the P2 *Headful AWT*). Four remain, and the honest
summary is:

| Row | Can wave 1 close it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Residual synthetic native set | **No, by design** | Its resolution is "classify every entry" — ~1330 of them — and both the row's own text and `stub_ratchet.rs`'s end-state gate defer that to wave 2 under contract §8 |
| Wholesale `Bridge` over-tagging | **Partly, and it moved today** | 227 shadows retired (`G90-1`). 943 remain; the two largest populations are `native-collections/src/lib.rs` (249) and `native-builtins/src/lib.rs` (161), the latter reserved by §8 |
| Duplicate dispatch implementations | **Premise is stale** | The "two lists that disagree" were centralised into one predicate and the exception retired on 2026-08-04 (`G86-1`, `G87-1`) |
| Synthetic-class fallback policy | **No** | Needs `native-builtins/src/lib.rs` |

**Contract §8 reads, verbatim:** *"Do not edit `native-builtins/src/lib.rs`; the
stub reclassification is a separate wave with its own subsystem-per-PR
discipline."* It names **one file**, not the crate — `G90-1` and the ratchet work
both touched other files under `native-builtins/` and `native-api/` without
crossing it.

### The trap that makes this row look closer than it is

`SyntheticStub` is **not** `allowed_in(JdkOnly)`
(`native-api/src/registry.rs:5268`). Every stub in the ratchet's count is
therefore already dropped under `--jdk-only`. **Retiring stubs lowers the
compatible-mode backlog and moves strict mode by exactly zero.** A falling stub
count is not strict-mode progress. What moves strict mode is retiring
`Bridge`-tagged **shadows** — §2.

## 2. The method that produced today's result, in the order to use it

This is the reusable part. It found 227 retirements in one session after the
same question had been argued from the census for two waves.

**Step 1 — measure the defect, split on outcome.**

```bash
cratonvm --java-home "$JDK" --jdk-only --jdk-only-report r.json -cp regression-suite/build RJdkStrict
```

`violations[]` where `kind == "native-shadows-bytecode"` carries an `outcome`
field: `bytecode-won` (the contract working) or `native-won` (**the defect**). A
count that does not split on it counts successes with failures — the `104` the
P0 row quotes is 27 + 77. Union all 36 `--jdk-only` vectors and it is **943**,
and that is a floor: 3 of 36 reports saturate the 256-entry sink.

**Step 2 — ask the dial, per prefix.** `CRATONVM_ENFORCE_NATIVE_SHADOW` takes a
comma-separated class-name prefix list and makes §1.4 enforced instead of
counted. The whole-VM result (`all`) is catastrophic and has been quoted as
settled for months; **it is not evenly distributed**. Measured 2026-08-19,
baseline 36/36:

```text
  all  1/36  ·  jdk/internal/access/  22/36  ·  java/security/  33/36
  java/net/ java/nio/channels/ sun/nio/ch/  34/36
  java/math/ javax/crypto/ javax/management/ javax/net/ssl/
    sun/security/ssl/ java/awt/image/ java/nio/file/  35/36
  java/text/ java/util/stream/ java/lang/module/
    java/util/concurrent/{atomic,locks}/ java/lang/ref/ sun/nio/fs/  36/36
```

The screen script is `scratchpad/screen.sh` in this session's temp dir; it is
twelve lines and worth re-creating rather than hunting for.

**Step 3 — census, to say WHICH rows.** The dial yields at dispatch when
bytecode is available; retirement drops the registration. They agree only where
bytecode exists. From `--dump-native-registry`, keep rows that are `bridge` **and**
`owns_slot` **and** `real_declaring_method.loaded` **and** `.has_code` **and not**
`.acc_native`. Under the seven clean prefixes that was 255 of 522.

**Step 4 — run the 102-vector arm. This is the acceptance test, and it is not
optional.** See §3.

## 3. The single most important thing in this document

**The 36-vector screen passed two prefixes the 102-vector arm rejected.**

`java/lang/ref/` and `sun/nio/fs/` were 36/36 on the screen. With all 255 in the
table the arm came back 99/102:

```text
  RFileTimes           lastModified  HotSpot 2021-01-01T00:00:00Z
                                     CratonVM 1601-01-02T20:42:25.920Z
  RClassUnloadSweep    payload.class.unloaded  HotSpot true / CratonVM false
  RClassUnloadSweepGen        "
```

1601 is the Windows FILETIME epoch — real `WindowsFileAttributes` bytecode read
its own time fields and found zeros, because the VM had been answering from side
state. The corpus of 36 has **no file-attribute vector and no class-unloading
vector**, so for those two subsystems it asked nothing and reported a pass.

That is the third instance of one defect found in a single day: a gate whose
stated population is wider than its measured one. The other two were the stub
ratchet running one of its two frozen configurations in CI (`G89-1` §4) and,
before that, twice inside the ratchet file itself. **It always reads as success.
Nothing catches it but running the wider thing.**

The corollary about *shape*: a `Reference` is four fields, a
`WindowsFileAttributes` a handful of longs. Both look exactly as stateless as an
`AtomicInteger`. "Stateless subsystems retire cleanly" is a place to look, never
a rule to apply — this wave was named after that rule and then had two sevenths
of it removed by measurement.

## 4. Instruments, and what each will lie to you about

| Instrument | Use it for | It will lie about |
|---|---|---|
| `--jdk-only-report` | the shadow population, refusal counters | **SUPERSEDED 2026-08-22.** All THREE bounded collections (`observation_sink`, `.jit_fastpath`, `.jit_compile`) now report `truncated` + `dropped`, and all three honour `CRATONVM_NATIVE_SHADOW_SINK_CAP` (default 4096; a value above the 65,536 ceiling CLAMPS and says so). A count is a floor only when `dropped > 0`. `run.sh` prints a three-way verdict — saturated / UNKNOWN / totals — and `jit_compile` rendered `null` until today, which the old `grep '"truncated": true'` read as "nothing truncated" on every strict run ever. See `H1-1` and `WORKER-5-NOTE-6`. |
| `--dump-native-registry` | `owns_slot`, `registered_by`, `real_declaring_method` | `invocations == 0` proves nothing (`G33-1`); `class-not-loaded` is the absence of a verdict, not a clean one (`G88-1`) |
| `stub_ratchet.rs` | the compatible-mode stub population | it froze ONE number until 2026-08-19; a rise can be a regression or an improvement, and only the row count separates them |
| the 36-vector screen | which prefixes deserve a 20-minute run | §3 |
| `regression-suite/run.sh` | **the verdict** | `SUITE` defaults to `core`; the three arms are three separate runs |

### The two-column rule (`G89-1` §2), because it is new and it works

A relabel keeps its registry row and moves only its kind. A new fake adds a row.
The ratchet now prints both, plus a per-file breakdown on every run, plus a
`CRATONVM_RATCHET_ROWS=1` row dump for diffing two commits by name. Today's
+226 came with the registry unchanged at 13160 rows, which adjudicated the whole
delta as relabelling without anyone having to remember what the change did.

## 5. Running the arms (get this wrong and you lose an hour)

```bash
TIMEOUT=420 JDK="C:/Program Files/Microsoft/jdk-25.0.3.9-hotspot" \
  CV="C:/craton/target-nolto/release/cratonvm.exe" \
  CRATONVM_ARGS="--jdk-only" bash regression-suite/run.sh
```

* `CV`, not `CRATONVM`. `JDK`, not `JAVA_HOME` (it reads `JAVA_HOME` as a
  fallback but defaults to a path that does not exist on this host).
* `TIMEOUT=420` is **mandatory** — `RMapGcStress` needs ~5 minutes.
* **Never run two arms at once.** `run.sh` uses a fixed temp path
  `regression-suite/.guard-tmp` and `rm -rf`s it per vector; two concurrent runs
  destroy each other's oracle files and produce a hundred false failures.
* Each arm takes 15–25 minutes, which is longer than a foreground tool call.
  Launch detached and poll the log for `REGRESSION SUITE:`.
* If a run is killed, **check for orphans before relaunching**:
  `Get-CimInstance Win32_Process | Where-Object { $_.CommandLine -match 'run\.sh' }`.
  Killing the parent leaves them, and they will eat the next run.

Build (5–15 min):

```bash
RUST_MIN_STACK=268435456 cargo build --release -p cratonvm-cli \
  --target-dir C:/craton/target-nolto --config 'profile.release.lto=false'
```

## 8. Standing traps, collected

* **A green arm cannot distinguish a safe retag from a no-op.** Five commits and
  four inflated totals were spent learning this (`G85-1`). Verify in three parts:
  the tag moved (registry dump), the behaviour holds (the arms), and the surface
  was actually exercised (non-zero invocations). Without the third, the second
  is vacuous.
* **Class identity is not state ownership.** 0 of 31 objects diverged on their
  real JDK class and `LinkedHashMap` still broke.
* **`class-not-loaded` is not a verdict.** Reading it as "nothing found" would
  have dropped 10 real JNI bridges in `instrument.rs`.
* **Rank retag candidates by the census fields, not by raw "not declared here"
  counts.** The registrar publicly called "where not to start" turned out to be
  among the safest — its 88 rows are all inherited-concrete.
* **Bare vector invocations are not the arm.** `run.sh` supplies per-vector
  launch args (`class_cv_args`, module-path flags); `RJdkForeign` and `RJdkModule`
  fail without them and pass under the arms.
* **Cite nothing from `feature-designs/jdk-only-mode.md` §8's stub count.** The
  `157` there is the root of a stale figure that propagated into three other
  documents. Re-derive.
